01 / ABOUT THE PROJECT

Security built for
constrained devices.

Zero-Trust IoT Sentinel is the flagship cybersecurity product being developed by Pufex, with an initial focus on Industrial IoT (IIoT) environments where low-cost devices cannot depend on heavyweight enterprise security agents or permanent cloud connectivity.

Its core market is industrial facilities and factories (IIoT), while the underlying Zero-Trust approach is also relevant to other critical connected environments such as hospitals and medical-device networks (IoMT), subject to domain-specific validation and adaptation.

02 / THE PROBLEM

One compromised node
can threaten the whole network.

Industrial IoT and Operational Technology environments need protection that works on small devices and can act locally—not just raise an alert.

01

Severe resource constraints

Low-power microcontrollers cannot easily run conventional endpoint detection agents, bulky antivirus software, or resource-heavy enterprise security suites.

02

Implicit trust & lateral movement

Flat networks can allow an attacker who compromises a camera or sensor to move toward controllers, databases, and other critical assets.

03

Weak device identities

IP and MAC addresses, as well as static keys stored in flash, may be copied, spoofed, or extracted from device memory.

04

Alerts without local action

Monitoring systems may detect suspicious activity but rely on cloud services or enterprise firewalls to enforce a response.

!

The central challenge — How can we continuously verify low-cost IoT devices and stop lateral movement in real time, without persistent cloud connectivity or heavy computational overhead?

03 / THE PROPOSED SOLUTION

Four pillars.
One defensive loop.

Sentinel connects device identity, access policy, network behavior, and response into a coordinated Zero-Trust workflow.

01

Identify

SRAM PUF + mTLS

Derive a device-specific secret from SRAM startup characteristics. A fuzzy extractor helps reproduce a stable secret, while nonce challenges support device verification over mTLS.

02

Authorize

Default-deny micro-segmentation

A Raspberry Pi gateway acts as the Policy Enforcement Point. Devices receive only the network access required for their role.

03

Monitor

Behavioral AI

The gateway collects network metadata—not encrypted payload contents—and sends selected behavioral features to a local Random Forest service.

04

Respond

Automated local isolation

When risk crosses a defined threshold, the system can revoke sessions and quarantine the node. An optional relay can cut device power when required.

Designed for offline resilience. Identity checks, policy decisions, AI inference, and containment are intended to run within the factory LAN without requiring a cloud round trip.

01

Enroll & authenticate

The ESP32 derives its SRAM PUF response and authenticates through the gateway using a challenge-response flow over mTLS.

02

Enforce least privilege

The gateway applies default-deny filtering. A device is allowed to communicate only with approved services and destinations.

03

Collect behavioral telemetry

Network metadata such as byte counters, transmission rate, and destination information is collected without inspecting payloads.

04

Evaluate device behavior

The local AI service processes prepared telemetry with the trained Random Forest model and returns a benign or malicious prediction.

05

Contain and alert

If the risk threshold is crossed, the backend instructs the gateway to revoke or isolate the node and raises an incident alert.

05 / DATASET & AI STRATEGY

Behavior over
device labels.

The AI component is designed to learn suspicious network behavior from prepared traffic telemetry, rather than relying on static device identifiers.

The proposal identifies the DataSense / CIC IIoT 2025 dataset as the initial source for model development and evaluation. Cleaning, preprocessing, and feature selection will be performed before Random Forest training.

07 / WHY SENTINEL

Designed around the
constraints that matter.

01

Hardware-rooted trust

Device identity is derived from silicon characteristics rather than relying only on identifiers or a static key stored in flash.

02

Local autonomy

Designed to keep verification, policy enforcement, AI evaluation, and response inside the local industrial network.

03

Constrained-device focus

Moves monitoring and enforcement to the gateway so the IoT node does not need to host a heavyweight security agent.

04

Containment, not alerts alone

Engineering considerations

SRAM PUF responses can vary with temperature and power-up conditions, so the fuzzy extractor and helper data require validation. The first prototype is planned for a small physical testbed with simulated attacks. Model performance on real gateway telemetry must also be validated. Secure OTA updates, automatic asset discovery, and formal protocol verification are identified as future work.
